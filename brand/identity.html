Higher Heights — Identity Draft 01 · October 2026 Film · Aerial · Photography

A new mark, a new wordmark, and the type system that follows from them. The old identity was a mountain zigzag above airy tracked capitals — a stock outdoor-brand device, and a wordmark that vanished below 40px. This one is built from one idea that the studio already owns: altitude.

01What was wrong
  • The mark was a generic mountain. A thin zigzag peak is the default icon for every drone, outdoor and travel brand. It said “outdoors”, not “commercial film”.
  • Mark and wordmark had no relationship. A 4px gold line drawing, then 60px capitals — different weights, different rhythm, floating apart.
  • The wordmark disappeared at small sizes. Ultra-wide tracking on thin geometric capitals means the name reads as a texture, not a name, in a nav bar or a favicon.
  • The gold tagline got lost on light backgrounds. It’s the least legible line in the logo on the one background it’s most often used on.
02The idea

One line that
gains height.

A horizon — then the same horizon, stepped up. It reads three ways at once: altitude (the ground rises), a cut (a film edit is a line moving forward), and a measurement (the tick marks on a lens or an altimeter).

It survives as a 16px favicon because it is only three rectangles. And it is not decoration applied to the page — the site already uses short gold hairlines beside every index number, so the mark is native to the layout.

03The system — four components
01 · Primary wordmarkInstrument Serif · opt. −0.010em

Primary wordmark

The name, set in a high-contrast serif at mixed case, tightened optically. This is the identity at its largest — hero, end cards, print, email sign-off. No symbol attached: at this quality the type is the logo.

Mark

The step alone. Used where the name is already known: favicon, social avatar, watermarks on footage, set stencils. Solid gold on navy, navy on cream — never outlined, never on a photo without a scrim.

Signature

Mark plus the name in tracked capitals — the version that stays legible at 22px in a nav bar. This is what the site header, invoices and email footers use.

Title card

The identity as it appears in the work itself: stacked capitals between two hairlines, tagline on the lower rule. Use it as the end card on every film and as the cover of any document — it is the one form that looks at home on screen.

04Type
Display — Instrument Serif 400
Have something
worth filming?

One family, roman and italic. The italic is no longer a foreign accent dropped into a sans page — it is the same hand, leaning.

Text — Instrument Sans 400 / 500
Commercial films, brand stories and aerial cinematography — made for clients who care how it looks.

Instrument Sans was drawn as the companion to Instrument Serif: same proportions, same skeleton. Set body at 400, labels at 500 with wide tracking.

Display / h1 · h2Instrument Serif 400−0.022em · line-height 1.02
Statements, quotesInstrument Serif Italic 400−0.02em
BodyInstrument Sans 400−0.005em · 1.62
Labels, nav, metaInstrument Sans 500+0.17em · uppercase · 10px
WordmarkInstrument Serif 400−0.010em · outlined

Why the logo forced this

The wordmark is a serif, so the display face had to become that serif — otherwise the logo and the headline are two different voices on the same screen. Once the display is a serif, the body wants a sans that belongs to it, and Geist does not: it is a tech grotesque, the face of dashboards and dev tools, with a flat, closed rhythm that fights a high-contrast serif.

Geist and Geist Mono are gone. The mono was doing the job of “technical label” — small tracked capitals — which Instrument Sans 500 does in the same voice as the rest of the page. Two families instead of three, and they were designed together.
